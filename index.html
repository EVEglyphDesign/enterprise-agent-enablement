A short note

The proof of concept worked. Let's help your people use it.

I'd like thirty minutes. Not to propose anything new — to talk about how we help the platform you already have find the hands it was built for.

What we set out to do, and what happened

The partnership deck we brought last December proposed an enterprise data mesh: a three-layer architecture spanning your SAP estate, Workday, Salesforce, and Bullhorn, with a semantic spine, a governed data fabric, and an AI access pattern delivered through Microsoft Teams. Phase 1 was strategy and architecture validation; Phase 2 was the foundation and lighthouse pilot on Contract-to-Cash.

We've done that work. Honestly, the proof of concept exceeded what we thought was possible when we scoped it. The historical reporting is all there. Delivering through Teams turned out to be straightforward. The AI agent answers cross-system questions safely, with the compliance controls the architecture promised. On our side we couldn't be happier with the result.

The problem we haven't solved yet

The gap isn't capability. The gap is that the people this platform was built for haven't had a real chance to use it yet. A working proof of concept is not the same thing as a tool your team reaches for on a Tuesday.

That last step is not another architecture question. It's a services question — who onboards which teams, in what order, with what kind of hand-holding, and what tells you it's working.

An Adoption Fitness Check

The shape I'd propose for the first six weeks is a short, structured read of where adoption actually stands. Four dimensions, one page each, one prioritized roadmap out the other side.

  1. Awareness. Do the named business users on the target teams know the mesh exists, what it can answer, and how to reach it from Teams? Awareness is the cheapest gap to close and often the one that has been assumed away.
  2. Confidence. When a user gets an answer from the agent, do they trust it enough to act on it — or do they still re-run the report in the old tool to check? Confidence is earned once, from a specific team, on a specific question. It doesn't generalize by accident.
  3. Fit. Does the platform answer the questions those specific teams actually ask on a Tuesday, or does it answer a slightly different set of questions the pilot was optimized for? Fit is a listening exercise before it is a build one.
  4. Habit. Six weeks in, are the same users coming back on their own, without a nudge? Are they bringing colleagues? Habit is the only dimension the platform team cannot cause from the outside. It is what tells you the work has actually landed.

The output is a prioritized roadmap — which team goes first, what hand-holding they need, what tells you six weeks in that it is working. It is not a re-architecture, and it is not a rebrand of the platform. It is the small, disciplined services motion that turns a delivered capability into a used one.

What I'd like to talk about

Thirty minutes. You tell me who inside your organization would benefit most if they were using the mesh next quarter, and what has kept them from it so far. I walk that named list through the four dimensions with you — briefly — and we agree on whether a fitness check of this shape is the right first step.

Nothing to prepare. Nothing to review. Just a call.
