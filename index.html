The last mile

Your agent is deployed. Your team isn't ready yet. Neither was mine.

That gap — between a capability that works and a team that runs it in production — is the loneliest place on an enterprise roadmap. The budget you have left has to land there. Everything after this page is about how we close it together, with people you'll actually get to know.

What I've seen at this edge

An agent gets stood up. It respects the semantic layer. On a good day it answers correctly against real data. On a hard day it sits idle because the people it was built for aren't yet sure when to trust it, when to override it, and what to keep as evidence when they do.

That's not a technology problem. It's a confidence problem, and confidence is built by the same specialists who built the capability — sitting with the team, in their tenant, on their calendar, on their real work — until the team owns it.

I've been the executive on the client side of this exact call. I know what it feels like to have a budget line to defend, a boss who backed you when it was still theoretical, and a team that needs the next twelve weeks to feel manageable. That's the ground this offer stands on.

Three lanes

01

Fractional resource pool

Named specialists on a monthly minimum. Between milestones, the same people remain accessible for guided hours — document review, process improvement, template refinement. The knowledge pool stays warm; the marginal cost of continuity is small.

02

Enterprise training & enablement

Curriculum for the client's own team on running the deployed agent safely in production. Secure-model workflows for sensitive lanes; open-model workflows for enterprise-standard information that should not be locked behind unnecessary security.

03

Reference-model standards

Public, versioned templates and frameworks the client's team forks and follows. Drift is not eliminated by discipline; it is limited by always going back to originals. Shared reference models mean independent users produce outputs that combine cleanly.

The motion

StageWhat happens
01Discovery, at the client's semantic layer. Walk the deployed agent with the client's team. What already runs? Which lanes are ready for production?
02Team readiness plan. Match specialists to lanes. Draft the curriculum, agree the monthly minimum, name the reference models the team will adopt.
03Enablement in production. Training and hands-on work happen inside the client's own tenant — client's calendar, client's document space, client's data. Nothing leaks into the delivery firm.
04Fractional operating rhythm. Monthly minimum shifts from training to guided operation. Specialists rotate in for specific asks, not blanket coverage.
05Steady state with surge rights. Client draws against the minimum for regular work and surges on the same specialists for milestones.

Why a small floor works better than a big project

A team of five during deployment doesn't stay a team of five in production, and paying for one that sits waiting is the fastest way to lose the room on next year's budget conversation. What you actually need is fewer people, the same ones, showing up on a rhythm your team can trust.

A monthly floor gives our specialists a reason to stay. It gives your team a predictable presence to lean on. And it gives you something clean to defend upstairs: a small, disciplined line that produces artifacts your auditors and your CFO can both read.

How the money works

  • Monthly floor — a fair, defensible line item. Named specialists plan against it; your team is expected to use the hours on real work, not filler.
  • Surge when it matters — same people, more hours, pre-agreed rates. Milestones don't force a new procurement cycle.

The exact numbers are a conversation between two executives, not a rate card. What I promise is that the specialists are paid well enough to keep showing up, the floor is small enough to be defensible, and nothing about the deal will surprise you six months in.

Reference-model discipline

Every artifact points back to an original. Templates are versioned. Forks declare their parent. Drift is limited by the discipline of always returning to the reference, not by policing what people produce.

The public reference models are hosted in this repository so the client's team, the specialists, and the commercial network all read from the same origin. This is what keeps outputs combining cleanly across independent users.
