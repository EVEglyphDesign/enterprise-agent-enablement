# The Last Mile — a Note from Jeff Eden

**For:** the executive on the client side of a deployed sovereign agent
**About:** what the enablement year could look like, and how we'd run it together

---

## Why I wrote this

I've spent a career building services businesses and taking them to market. I've also been the executive who backed a team into a hard new capability and needed a partner who wouldn't disappear once the invoice was signed. This note is written from that seat — not from a sales one.

Your agent is deployed. It mirrors your system of record and respects the semantic layer. On a good day it answers correctly against real data. The gap between that and production isn't a technology gap. It's the year your team needs to learn when to trust it, when to override it, and what to keep as evidence when they do.

That last mile is what I want to walk with you.

---

## What I'd bring to the table

- **Named specialists — the ones who built the capability.** Not a staffing pool. A short bench of practitioners you'll know by first name — the same people your team can call directly.
- **A monthly floor, not a project.** Predictable presence, disciplined hours. Small enough for you to defend to your CFO, real enough for the team to feel every week.
- **Time inside your tenant.** Training, document review, hands-on work — all on your calendar, in your document space, against your data. Nothing leaves the room.
- **Portable reference models.** Versioned templates and frameworks so if we ever part ways, your team continues on the same foundation without a rebuild.
- **Me, personally, on the executive line.** You get me for the conversation, not a handoff. If anything starts to feel wrong, you have my number.

---

## The year I'd propose

**Weeks 1–4 — Standing beside your team.** We walk the deployed agent together — which lanes already run, which lanes need training first, where the trust is thin. Not an assessment. A short, honest read.

**Weeks 5–12 — Enablement in production.** Curriculum on your calendar. Secure-model workflows for the sensitive lanes; open-model workflows for the enterprise-standard information that shouldn't be locked behind unnecessary security. Same frameworks, same templates, same origin documents — so nobody is reinventing on the third floor.

**Weeks 13–24 — Fractional operating rhythm.** The floor stops being about training and becomes about guided operation. Document review, process improvement, template refinement, occasional deep-dives. Specialists rotate in on specific asks; the pool stays warm without a full team sitting idle.

**Weeks 25 onward — Steady state with surge rights.** You draw against the floor for regular work and surge on the same specialists for milestones. No new procurement cycle when the next quarter's demand shows up.

---

## What I promise about the money

The exact numbers are a conversation between us, not a rate card. What I'll commit to now:

- The specialists are paid well enough to keep showing up. Continuity is the whole product; underpaying it defeats the offer.
- The floor is small enough that you can defend it upstairs without a fight. This is not priced to look impressive on a slide.
- Nothing about the deal will surprise you six months in. If a milestone needs more, we agree on the surge before it happens, at rates you already saw.

If any of that doesn't sound right for where you are, I'd rather hear it now.

---

## How my network fits in

Once an account is steady, I bring in commercial partners from my own network — people I trust to run the recurring service alongside me, extending the pool without diluting who you deal with day-to-day. That's how a fractional pool scales without turning into an anonymous staffing firm.

You still deal with me at the executive line. That doesn't change.

---

Thirty minutes, no deck. You tell me where the year's budget has to land and what a good next twelve months looks like for your team. I'll tell you honestly whether this offer fits — and if it doesn't, what would.
