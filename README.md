# Enterprise Agent Enablement — The Last Mile

For enterprise teams whose sovereign agent is already deployed and now needs a year of enablement to reach production.

**Public surface:** https://eveglyphdesign.github.io/enterprise-agent-enablement/
**Led by:** Jeff Eden — builder, services executive, and executive counterpart on the client's line. [linkedin.com/in/jeffeden](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jeffeden)

## What this is

The agent is not the deliverable. The trained team is.

An agent that mirrors the system of record and respects the semantic layer is one step short of production. The step that closes it is enablement — the client's own team learning when to trust the agent, when to override it, and what to keep as evidence when they do.

This surface is written from Jeff's seat as a services executive who has stood at this edge before. It describes a fractional pool of named specialists, priced against a small monthly floor, running inside the client's own tenant — designed so the executive who signs the line item can defend it in twelve months as easily as they signed it today.

## Three lanes

1. **Fractional resource pool** — named specialists (SAP, analytics, process, document review, change management) available on a monthly minimum. Between milestones the same people remain accessible for guided hours — document review, process improvement, template refinement — so the knowledge pool stays warm and the marginal cost of continuity is small.
2. **Enterprise training & enablement** — curriculum for the client's own team on how to use the deployed agent safely in production. Secure-model workflows for sensitive lanes; open-model workflows for enterprise-standard information that should not be locked behind unnecessary security. Same frameworks, same templates, same reference documents — every user reads from the same origin.
3. **Reference-model standards** — public, versioned templates and frameworks that the client's team forks and follows. Drift is not eliminated by discipline; it is limited by always going back to originals. Shared reference models mean independent users produce outputs that combine cleanly.

## Why fractional

A project team of five during deployment does not stay a project team of five in production. The client's real need is a smaller monthly draw on the same people, plus the ability to surge on demand. Fractional access to the specialist pool preserves the knowledge that was accumulated during deployment, without paying a full team to sit and wait.

The minimum-usage floor exists so specialists can plan against it, and so the client is expected to spend the hours purposefully — document review, process improvement, a few hours a week guiding the team.
